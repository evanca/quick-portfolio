I worked at Siemens for five years across several teams as part of the University of Cincinnati co-op program. This exposed me to a wide range of development environments, from large-scale enterprise software to experimental R&D projects involving VR, simulation and machine learning.

### Full-time role (post-graduation)

After graduating, I joined the **Teamcenter Classification team** as a full-time engineer. Working alongside some incredibly knowledgeable engineers gave me a deeper appreciation for the complexity of large enterprise systems and the importance of strong architecture and data modeling. I worked full-stack within the Teamcenter ecosystem and gained experience with modern frontend frameworks and enterprise data management practices.

**Technical skills used**: C++, JSON Schema, React, Angular

### Advanced Solutions and R&D work

During my co-op rotations I worked on a variety of experimental projects exploring simulation, VR/AR development and machine learning:

- A **VR fluid simulation prototype** using NVIDIA FleX
- Real-time particle simulations and wind tunnel visualizations in Unreal Engine
- **Weak supervision machine learning** using Snorkel
- Training an ML model with **Unity ML Agents** for visual contrast recognition in AR environments
- Physics engines within Unity-based VR/AR applications

**Technical skills used**: C#, Unity, Unreal Engine 4, Python, Snorkel, NVIDIA GameWorks

### Early co-op work

Earlier rotations focused on development within Siemens NX and related tooling. I worked on lattice structure generation, which lets parts keep their structural integrity while using far less material, and developed adaptive algorithms that cut lattice generation time from minutes to seconds on large models.

This is where I was first introduced to 3D printing and additive manufacturing, and where I saw how software development can directly change physical manufacturing.

**Technical skills used**: C++, Python, VBA, 3D printing
